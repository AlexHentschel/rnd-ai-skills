# Effective Behavioral Guidelines

Compressed calibration for rule-writing. Receiver shares vocabulary and disposition; what follows is the accumulated sense of where directives quietly fail.

## Core structure

Every directive lives on an axis from **pure fact** (state description, no verb, no behaviour change) to **pure procedure** (committed shape: exact steps, words, artifacts). Neither endpoint is a behavioural guideline. Effective directives sit in the middle and carry three things:

- **Target** — the outcome the rule exists to produce.
- **Evaluate cue** — the question the rule should fire, in the moment it applies.
- **Act cue** — smallest nudge toward the target; not a full procedure.

A directive missing any of the three is a fact-record pretending to be a rule. Target + constraints leave shape recoverable per-situation; shape committed today is based on incomplete information about situations the rule will actually meet, and is expensive to undo once baked into plans, templates, habits, cross-references.

## The in-moment pull

Prescription is seductive while drafting because decisive phrasing feels more actionable, testable, and compresses well — `always do X` is one line, `when you notice Y, consider whether Z serves the target` is three. The compression is false economy: the over-specific rule ages into friction as soon as situations diverge from the originating one. Treat `always / never / must / step 1` openings as red flags and ask: am I protecting target, or committing shape?

## Four questions before writing any directive

1. **Goal** — what outcome? Unstated goal → unfalsifiable rule → untestable, un-drift-detectable, un-retirable.
2. **Evaluate cue** — what question should this prompt, in the moment?
3. **Act cue** — smallest nudge, not a procedure.
4. **Scope** — task / project / user / universal? Apply at exactly that scope. On import across contexts, re-check the assumptions that made the rule valid at origin (authority structure, risk profile, maturity, collaboration phase). Assumptions often fail to transfer even when language does.

## Failure modes

| Symptom | Root | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| Bullet reads as procedure (`do X then Y then Z`) | Shape committed before the situation was known | State target + evaluate cue; list steps as labelled *illustrative example*, not mandate |
| Bullet is facts with no verb | Recorded state, not behaviour | Add trigger / evaluate / act structure |
| Multiple rules share one underlying mechanism | Abstraction missed | Lift to a principle; move the incidents to a Notes column as dated evidence |
| Rule is principle *and* procedure mashed together | Purpose conflation — reader can't tell which part is authoritative | Split: principle in rule text, procedure as labelled example |
| Register drifts *might → should → must → always* without flag | Hypothesis quietly became prescription | Use markers: `(experimental)`, `(hypothesis)`, *illustrative not exhaustive*, *starting point for critical evaluation* |
| Prescription share exceeds ~20–30% of the ruleset | Over-committed set | Audit |

## Abstraction lifecycle

1. **New incident** → record specifically.
2. **Second similar incident** → still specific; note the repeat.
3. **Third incident, different enough that the specific wording no longer fits** → lift to generalised principle; incidents move to Notes as dated evidence. Nothing is lost; only description level changes.
4. **Clean application of the principle to a fresh situation outside the originating set** → promote from `(experimental)` to `established`. Two independent clean applications is a reasonable bar.

**Stopping rule**: stop one level *below* either (a) an articulable counterexample at the next level, or (b) the level where evaluate/act cues stop suggesting a concrete next move. Over-generalisation and under-generalisation are mirror failures; both destroy evaluability.

## Prescription's legitimate niches

Tool with measure, not default. Accept prescription for:

- **Stated preferences** (formats, conventions) where the learning is already in hand; general principles would be noise.
- **Mechanical work** with no design latitude (renames, already-decided replacements).
- **Repeatedly-reinforced behaviours** — reinforcement history earns emphasis, but keep framing universally-applicable in content even when emphatic in tone.
- **Catastrophic-if-violated invariants** — precision beats flexibility where breach means data loss or silent corruption.

## Two companions

- **Contradictions have no default winner.** New-is-right and old-is-right are mirror biases. When new findings conflict with existing rules, present both sides with evidence, sources, and reproduction conditions; deciding authority picks; retain the losing side with a dated note so decision history survives.
- **Reflect audibly at checkpoints.** At every phase close, loop iteration, milestone, state out loud either "here's what I'd revise" or "nothing changes, continuing". Silence masks skipped reflection — looks the same from outside, costs compounding drift inside.

## Distillation

A directive that ages well describes **what outcome** and **how to recognise its applicability**, not **what to type**. When in doubt, protect target; leave shape to the situation.
